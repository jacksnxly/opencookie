The one-line drop-in is the fastest way to start, and it is deliberately small:
it carries a single purpose and its English copy. A site that needs several
purposes, or a banner in another language, does not need a bundler to get them.
Browsers resolve bare module specifiers through an
[import map](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/script/type/importmap),
so static HTML can load the same modules a bundled application does.

Copy the two packages into the site, map the four specifiers, and mount:

The page reads the policy you published. `opencookie policy starter` writes
that document and `opencookie policy publish` records it, so serving
`opencookie/policy.json` beside the page keeps the banner and the published
policy the same thing. Nothing here is compiled: it is the same module the
bundled routes import, loaded directly.

What this route gives you that the drop-in does not:

- **Every purpose your policy defines.** The preferences surface lists them all,
  each with its own label and disclosure.
- **Any language.** Pass `VANILLA_COPY_DE` for German, or your own strings for
  another language. No copy is bundled that your visitors will not read.
- **The full programmatic API**, including custom resource controllers and a
  server-backed decision store.

<Aside type="caution">
  An import map cannot carry an `integrity` attribute, so a module loaded
  through one is not pinned the way the drop-in's `integrity` pin is. Serve
  these files from your own origin rather than a public CDN, and they are
  covered by the same deployment you already control.
</Aside>

<Aside type="note">
  The four specifiers must all be mapped. `@opencookie-dev/vanilla` imports
  `@opencookie-dev/core` and two of its subpaths, and a browser resolves each one
  through the map.
</Aside>
